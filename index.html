Public Transit and Commute Inequality in New York City

This project compares census tracts in the Bronx and Manhattan to examine whether similar levels of public-transit use are associated with different commuting outcomes.

Research Question

Do Bronx census tracts experience longer commute times than Manhattan census tracts even though public-transit use is similar?

Data

I use the 2011–2015 American Community Survey 5-year estimates from the U.S. Census Bureau. The final analysis contains 602 census tracts: 327 in the Bronx and 275 in Manhattan.

Method

The analysis is descriptive. I compare average commute time and public-transit use across census tracts in the Bronx and Manhattan. I also compare several socioeconomic characteristics, including poverty, unemployment, household income, and working from home.

Each census tract receives equal weight in the analysis. The results therefore describe differences between the average census tract in each borough rather than the commute experience of the average individual worker.

Commute Time

Bronx census tracts have an average commute time of 42.63 minutes, compared with 30.80 minutes in Manhattan. This produces an observed difference of 11.83 minutes.

Relative to the Manhattan tract average, the Bronx tract average is approximately 38% higher.

Public-Transit Use

Public-transit use is much more similar. Approximately 59.05% of workers in Bronx census tracts use public transportation, compared with 58.14% in Manhattan census tracts.

That is a difference of only 0.91 percentage points, even though the commute-time difference is almost twelve minutes.

Main Finding

The two boroughs have almost identical levels of public-transit use, but very different average commute times. This suggests that transit mode share alone does not fully describe transportation burden.

Socioeconomic Context

The Bronx and Manhattan census tracts also differ substantially in several socioeconomic characteristics. These comparisons provide additional context for the commute-time difference, but they do not establish that these factors cause the commute gap.

Poverty and Unemployment

Average tract poverty is 29.59% in the Bronx compared with 17.56% in Manhattan. Average unemployment is also higher in Bronx tracts, at 13.91%, compared with 7.64% in Manhattan.

Working From Home

Working from home is less common in the Bronx census tracts. The average is 3.27% in the Bronx compared with 6.85% in Manhattan. This is relevant because access to work-from-home opportunities may affect who needs to commute regularly.

Household Income

The median across tract-level median household income estimates is $33,095 in the Bronx and $81,250 in Manhattan.

These differences demonstrate that commuting takes place within very different socioeconomic environments. However, this descriptive analysis cannot determine how much income, poverty, unemployment, or working from home contributes to the observed commute-time difference.

Comparing the Two Main Gaps

This graph highlights the central result of the project. The average commute time differs by 11.83 minutes between the two boroughs, while average public-transit use differs by only 0.91 percentage points.

The comparison shows why public-transit use and commuting burden should be treated as different measures.

Takeaway

Transportation inequality cannot be understood only by asking whether people use public transportation. Bronx and Manhattan census tracts have nearly identical average transit-use rates but substantially different average commute times.

The results also show major socioeconomic differences between the two groups of census tracts. Future research could examine station locations, service frequency, transfers, employment locations, and the number of jobs accessible from different neighborhoods.

These results are descriptive and do not establish a causal relationship between neighborhood characteristics, transportation systems, and commute time.

Data source: U.S. Census Bureau, 2011–2015 American Community Survey 5-year estimates.
